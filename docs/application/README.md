## Project summary (120 words)

ContinuumBench measures what a small or medium-sized organisation pays, in accuracy, latency and energy, for running language models under its own control. On one Apple M1 Max I benchmarked six quantised configurations of Qwen2.5 and Mistral-7B on 450 healthcare, digital-industries and general-science questions, on the GPU and on the CPU as an emulated edge server, with measured component power. Energy per request ranged from about 1 J to 19 J, and the EU-origin 7B model cost as much as the most accurate one while scoring like a model five times smaller. A pre-registered confidence cascade saved 29% of the large model's energy for a 2.2-point accuracy loss, a tentative result. A six-dimension sovereignty scorecard makes the value choices explicit.

## Motivation paragraph (200 words)

At HelloPM I wrote routing logic that sent course questions and customer-service requests to different paths behind a third-party LLM API. It worked, but I could not say what each path cost or where the data went. ContinuumBench is my attempt to ask properly. I froze a protocol before collecting data, set the routing thresholds from a pilot, and measured energy with powermetrics rather than estimating it. The results were more humbling than I expected: the small models were often confidently wrong, so routing saved about a third of the energy, not most of it, and the oracle showed how much headroom better confidence estimation could unlock. I also learned that "run it at the edge" is not automatically the frugal choice, and that a sovereignty score is only as neutral as its rubric. This vacancy's combination of sustainable inference, the computing continuum and digital sovereignty is the question I kept running into. I want to extend this from one laptop to real edge hardware and hosted European tiers, and to work on the confidence and verification methods that decide whether routing can pay off. I built this with AI coding assistance, disclosed, and I can defend every design choice.

## Interview defence pack

Short, honest answers to the questions a sceptical supervisor is likely to ask.

**Why one laptop? Isn't that too narrow?**
It is narrow, and the report says so. The aim was a calibrated first data point with a reusable method: frozen protocol, per-item records, measured energy with provenance. One machine removes cross-hardware confounds; the next step is real edge devices and a hosted tier.

**powermetrics is not wall-plug energy. Why trust it?**
It reports CPU, GPU and ANE rails, not DRAM, display or PSU losses, so absolute numbers are a lower bound. Comparisons between configurations on the same chip are what the study relies on. Idle power (1.48 W) was subtracted, and it is small against 12 to 51 W cell power, so baseline choice moves results by a few percent at most.

**Your edge tier is the same chip. What does that show?**
Only CPU versus GPU on this hardware. It is labelled "measured on an emulated tier" everywhere. The finding is narrow but useful: placement on weaker hardware is not automatically cheaper.

**H3 passed on point estimates but the CI crosses the tolerance. Is it supported?**
Tentatively. The policy was chosen on validation and evaluated once on 135 test items. The accuracy interval runs to minus 5.9 points against a 5-point tolerance, so I report it as tentative rather than confirmed. A larger test set is the obvious fix.

**Why did routing save so little compared with the oracle?**
The small model was confidently wrong often enough that the cascade had to escalate most requests to stay near the large model's accuracy. Better calibration, task-aware features or a verifier are where the gap is.

**Two cells swapped. Did you drop data?**
By the pre-registered rule, cells with memory page-outs are excluded from latency and energy, and their accuracy is kept. Both were the 1.5B model on the CPU tier.

**Is the sovereignty score objective?**
No, and it is not meant to be. It scores the deploying organisation's control. Ranks were stable across Dirichlet-sampled weights because under this rubric local options dominate the hosted API on every dimension. A different rubric (for example, publisher jurisdiction) could reverse that; the point is to make the values explicit.

**What did the AI assistant do, and what did you do?**
I set the brief and constraints, made the go/no-go calls, and ran every measurement on my own Mac. The assistant proposed the protocol and wrote most of the code and drafts under my direction. Every number is generated by code from committed raw data, and every reference was checked against Crossref or arXiv.

**What would you do first in the PhD?**
Replace the emulated edge tier with real edge hardware, add a hosted EU tier with documented energy assumptions, and test whether better confidence estimation closes the cascade's gap to the oracle.
