\textbf{What an SME can take from this.} On one laptop, the choice of model moved energy per request by more than an order of magnitude while moving accuracy by tens of points, and the two did not move together in a simple way: the EU-origin 7B model cost as much as the most accurate model but answered fewer questions correctly than a model five times smaller. Measured energy, not parameter count, is what separates these options. The same holds for placement. Moving a small model from the laptop GPU to its CPU, a stand-in for a modest on-premise server, made it slower and costlier at equal accuracy. ``Running it at the edge'' is therefore not automatically the sustainable choice; it depends on the hardware the edge actually has.

\textbf{Why routing helped only modestly.} Routing pays off when a cheap model knows when it is likely to be wrong. Here the small model was often confidently wrong, so the cascades had to escalate most requests to stay near the large model's accuracy, and the savings shrank from what the small model alone offers to roughly a third. The gap between the cascades and the oracle shows where the leverage is: better confidence estimation, task-aware routing features or a verifier would convert more of that headroom into savings \cite{guo2017,kadavath2022,hybridllm}. The pre-registered result is a useful calibration for SMEs: routing is worth a meaningful fraction of energy, not most of it, at today's small-model calibration.

\textbf{Sovereignty as a design decision.} The scorecard's stable ranking came from the rubric, not from the data: once self-hosting is scored as the organisation's own control, every local option beats any hosted one regardless of weights, and the EU-origin model is not favoured. A different organisation could reasonably score publisher jurisdiction, training-data transparency or supply-chain origin more finely and reach a different ranking. We think this is the right way to use such an instrument: as a transparent statement of which dimensions an organisation chooses to value, with evidence attached, rather than as a single objective number. The static sensitivity rule makes the trade-off concrete: honouring a preference for an EU-origin model on sensitive items cost accuracy without saving much energy.

\textbf{What one laptop cannot show.} The continuum in this study is short: one GPU, its own CPU and a documented but unexecuted cloud tier. The figures say nothing about data-centre efficiency, batching servers or network energy, and the edge tier shares the chip with the device tier. The contribution is a method and a calibrated first data point that a larger study can extend to real edge hardware and hosted tiers.
