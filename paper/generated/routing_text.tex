Routing energy is estimated, not measured: each item is priced at the measured mean energy per request of its configuration and domain, and a cascade pays for both models on escalated items. Table~\ref{tab:routing} reports all policies on the 135 test items. Always using Qwen2.5-1.5B saved 79\% of energy at -4.4 points. The confidence cascades and the learned router sat in between: the margin cascade saved 29\% at -2.2 points, the $p_{\max}$ cascade 28\% at -1.5, and the learned router 34\% at -3.0. The tuned cascade thresholds were high ($\tau=0.99$ and 0.98), and the cascades escalated 45\% and 44\% of test items, because the small model is often confidently wrong. Compared with always using the small model, the cascades buy back about 2 points of accuracy for about 50 percentage points of the saving. The constraint-aware optimiser chose Qwen2.5-7B in every domain: no cheaper single configuration stayed within 5 points of it on validation. The static sensitivity rule, which keeps sensitive items on the EU-origin model, saved 12\% and lost 8.1 points: here sovereignty preferences cost accuracy rather than energy. An oracle that knew which model would be right reached 88.1\% at 68\% saving; it is unattainable, but it shows the headroom that better confidence signals could unlock.

\textbf{H3.} The learned router's weights were fit on train; every threshold was tuned on validation, and the H3 policy was then selected on the same validation split, so validation was used twice and test once. Selected this way, the cascade margin policy saved 29.3\% of estimated energy per request on test (95\% interval 20 to 38\%) with an accuracy change of -2.2 points [-5.9, +1.5]. Both point estimates meet the thresholds set before confirmatory data, so H3 is supported on point estimates. Neither interval clears its threshold: the saving interval reaches below 25\% and the accuracy interval past the 5-point tolerance. Two further checks point the same way. The test split appears easier for the small model than the others (relative to Qwen2.5-7B, always Qwen2.5-1.5B scored -9.3, -14.4 and -4.4 points on train, validation and test; the selected cascade -3.6, -4.4 and -2.2), so the test loss is probably an underestimate. And if each cell's energy is apportioned by item wall time instead of evenly, the saving becomes 24.6\%. We therefore read H3 as tentative support at best, and as a calibrated estimate of what this kind of cascade is worth rather than a confirmed effect.
