Table~\ref{tab:mac} and Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} summarise RQ1. Qwen2.5-7B (4-bit, GPU) was the most accurate configuration at 74.7\% [70.7, 78.7] and cost 18.3\,J of measured incremental energy per request. Qwen2.5-1.5B (4-bit, GPU) reached 65.8\% at 3.8\,J, about 5 times less energy for 8.9 points lower accuracy. Mistral-7B (4-bit) scored 65.3\%, indistinguishable from Qwen2.5-1.5B (paired difference -0.4 points, 95\% interval -5.1 to +4.2), at an energy cost similar to Qwen2.5-7B. The 0.5B models were close to chance on several subjects (37.3\%). 8-bit quantisation did not improve accuracy over 4-bit at 1.5B (64.4\% vs 65.8\%) and did not cost more energy (3.4 vs 3.8\,J). Domain matters: Qwen2.5-7B scored 91.3\% on the general-science control but 58.7\% on healthcare, where Qwen2.5-1.5B did slightly better (62.7\%). On the emulated CPU edge tier (4 of the chip's 10 CPU cores), the same 0.5B 4-bit model gave the same accuracy but took 5.6 times longer per request (median 160 vs 29\,ms) and 2.2 times the energy: the 8-bit 0.5B model showed the same pattern, so on this laptop and with this thread count the GPU was both faster and more frugal than the CPU. Idle power was 1.48\,W (1.25 and 1.70\,W in the first and last 30\,s), small against mean cell power of 12 to 51\,W, so the choice of idle window changes cell energy by at most 2.2\%. Clock alignment matters more for short cells: power-sample timestamps have one-second resolution, and shifting them by half a second changes energy by up to 8.3\% for the 0.5B cells, 3.3\% for 1.5B and under 1\% for the 7B models. 2 of 27 cells recorded system-wide memory page-outs during the cell and are excluded from latency and energy by the pre-registered rule (qwen1.5b-q4 edge digital industries, qwen1.5b-q4 edge healthcare); their accuracy is kept. Repeated timings were stable (median coefficient of variation 0.5\%).
